Your coding agent gets what you said, next to what was on screen.

ScreenBrief is for developers who build iPhone apps with a coding agent. Record your screen while you talk about what you want changed. The agent reads it as a timeline, then makes the changes.

  1. 01

    Record

    Use your app on the iPhone and say what you want changed.

  2. 02

    Copy

    Open the recording in ScreenBrief and tap Copy.

  3. 03

    Paste

    Paste into your agent's thread. Away from your Mac, attach the video from Photos too.

What your agent gets

The agent runs one command on your Mac and gets a timeline: what you said, next to screenshots of what was on screen at that moment. One screenshot for each spoken phrase and each screen change.

How it works

Two parts: an iPhone app that records, and a Mac command-line tool your agent runs. There is no Mac app with a window.

iPhone

ScreenBrief app, Swift

  1. Record the whole screen and the microphone, in any app
  2. Save the video to Photos
  3. Transcribe on the phone
  4. Copy
paste

Thread

where you talk to your agent

  1. The pasted text: a recording id, one command, your transcript
  2. The video too, if you are away from the Mac
agent runs one command

Mac

screenbrief pull <id>

  1. Fetch the video from the iPhone or from the thread
  2. Transcribe with Whisper
  3. Pick a screenshot for each spoken phrase and each screen change
  4. Write FEEDBACK.md and the frames
FEEDBACK.md

Agent

your coding agent

  1. Read the timeline
  2. Open the frames
  3. Make the changes

Two ways to get the video

From the iPhone over Xcode's device pairing, when the phone is unlocked and on the same Wi-Fi as the Mac or plugged in. Or from a video you attached to the thread in T3 Code.

No guessing

If the agent cannot get the video, the pasted text tells it to wait and ask you. It is told not to work from the transcript alone.

Any agent that can run a command

It was built for T3 Code threads. The pasted text is plain, so any agent that can run a shell command on your Mac can use it.

Optional touch overlay

iOS does not show touches in a screen recording. A debug-only overlay for the app you are testing (React Native or SwiftUI) draws taps and finger trails so they show.

What you need and how to install

  • A Mac with Xcodeto build the app and reach the phone
  • An iPhoneand your own Apple developer team
  • Homebrewfor ffmpeg and whisper.cpp
  • Node 24the tool has no npm dependencies

Step 1

Set up the Mac tool

git clone https://github.com/RostyslavDzhohola/screenbrief.gitcd screenbriefbrew install ffmpeg whisper-cppln -s "$PWD/mac/bin/screenbrief" /opt/homebrew/bin/screenbriefscreenbrief doctor

The fourth line puts mac/bin/screenbrief on your PATH. screenbrief doctor checks ffmpeg, whisper.cpp, the Whisper model and Xcode's device tool, and says what is missing.

Step 2

Build the iPhone app

Build the app in ios/ with Xcode and install it on your iPhone. Set your own team and bundle ids first. It is not on the App Store.

The README has the details: where the Whisper model goes, which signing settings to change, and every command.

Privacy

Everything runs on your own iPhone and Mac. Transcription is on-device on the phone and local on the Mac.

Nothing is uploaded to a ScreenBrief server, because there is none. Your agent sees the text and frames you give it, like anything else in the thread.

Limits and what is in progress

A personal tool made public. It is early.

Today

  • iPhone and Mac only.
  • You build the iPhone app yourself with Xcode. There is no packaged download.
  • Portrait recordings.
  • No touches in the recording, unless the app you are testing includes the overlay.
  • A video you attach in T3 Code can be up to 50 MB, about 8 minutes. Recordings are about 5.6 MB a minute.

Being built In progress

  • Splitting recordings longer than 8 minutes into parts that fit the attachment limit.
  • Fetching the video automatically later, when the phone was out of reach.
